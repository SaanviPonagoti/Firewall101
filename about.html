About the Framework

What incident response is, why colleges need it, and what this framework aims to achieve.

What is cybersecurity incident response?

Cybersecurity incident response is the organised approach an institution takes when something goes wrong in its digital environment, such as a phishing attack, malware infection, stolen device or data leak. Instead of reacting in panic, people follow agreed steps, roles and timelines so the problem is found quickly, limited in size, fixed properly and learned from.

This framework is based on the NIST incident-response lifecycle (NIST SP 800-61) and adapted for the everyday realities of a college campus.

Why educational institutions need it

Colleges hold valuable data and run open, shared networks, which makes them attractive targets.

Student records

Admissions, grades, IDs and personal details must stay private and accurate.

Staff data

HR files, payroll and credentials of faculty and administrators.

Research data

Unpublished findings, projects and intellectual property.

Financial information

Fee payments, scholarships, vendor accounts and budgets.

Learning systems

LMS, online exams, e-library and email that classes depend on.

Campus networks

Wi-Fi, labs and servers shared by thousands of users and devices.

Main goals

  • Detect incidents early through awareness, monitoring and easy reporting.
  • Reduce damage by containing threats quickly.
  • Restore services safely so teaching and administration continue.
  • Protect sensitive data belonging to students, staff and the college.
  • Improve future response by learning from every incident.
Based on NIST

The NIST lifecycle

Preparation → Detection & Analysis → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Post-Incident Review.
