Phishing Email

Typical severity: Low
  1. Do not click links or open attachmentsLeave the email as it is. Do not reply, and do not forward it to others.
  2. Take a screenshotCapture the full message, including the sender address and any links (do not click them).
  3. Report the emailSend the screenshot to the IT Help Desk or use the incident report form.
  4. Never share passwords or OTPsNo genuine college service will ask for them by email, call or chat. If you already clicked or entered details, change your password immediately and say so in your report.
  5. IT reviews and warns usersThe IT team checks the message, blocks the sender or link, and sends a warning to students and staff if it is spreading.

Ransomware

Typical severity: Critical
  1. Disconnect the device from the networkUnplug the network cable and turn off Wi-Fi. Do not shut down unless told to, as this can destroy evidence.
  2. Do not pay and do not delete filesPaying does not guarantee recovery and encourages attackers. Leave the ransom note and encrypted files untouched.
  3. Report immediatelyCall the Cybersecurity Incident Response Team and the IT Help Desk right away (see Contact).
  4. Preserve evidencePhotograph the screen, note the time and what you were doing, and keep the device untouched for investigators.
  5. Remove the threat firstThe IR team identifies the malware, closes the entry point and cleans or re-images affected systems.
  6. Restore from clean backups onlyRestore data only after the threat is fully removed, and verify the backups are clean before reconnecting systems.

Lost or Stolen Device

Typical severity: Medium to High
  1. Report immediatelyTell the IT Help Desk as soon as you notice. If stolen, also inform campus security.
  2. Remotely lock or wipe if possibleUse device-finder or college device-management tools to lock, locate or wipe the device.
  3. Change your passwordsReset college email, LMS and any other accounts that were signed in on the device.
  4. Check for unauthorised accessIT reviews account sign-in logs and sessions for activity after the time of loss.
  5. Inform the Data Protection OfficerRequired if the device held sensitive data such as student records, staff data or research files.

Other incidents: quick guidance

Full playbooks for these can be added as the framework grows. Until then, take these first actions and report.

Malware infection

Disconnect from the network, stop using the device, run no "clean-up" tools on your own, and report to IT.

Password / account compromise

Change your password from a safe device, enable two-factor authentication, sign out of all sessions and report.

Data breach

Report at once, do not try to delete or "fix" data, and let the Data Protection Officer lead notification decisions.

Denial-of-service attack

Report slowdowns to IT; the network team filters traffic and works with the internet provider if needed.

Website defacement

Do not edit the page. Take a screenshot, report to IT, and the web team will take the site offline and restore a clean copy.
