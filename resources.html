Cybersecurity awareness

Use strong passwords

Use long, unique passphrases (12+ characters). Never reuse your college password elsewhere.

Enable two-factor authentication

A second step such as an app code stops most account takeovers even if your password leaks.

Do not click unknown links

Check the sender and hover over links first. When in doubt, go to the website directly.

Do not share OTPs or passwords

No one from the college, bank or IT team will ever ask for your OTP or password.

Keep software updated

Install operating system, browser and app updates promptly to close known security holes.

Back up important files

Keep copies of assignments and projects on cloud storage or an external drive.

Frequently asked questions

I clicked a suspicious link. What should I do?

Disconnect from the network if you can, change your password from another safe device, and report it immediately. Reporting quickly matters more than embarrassment.

Will I get in trouble for reporting an incident?

No. Good-faith reports are encouraged and help protect everyone. The aim is to fix problems, not to blame people.

How do I know if an email is really from the college?

Check the sender's full address, look for urgency or odd requests, and confirm with the IT Help Desk through a known contact method if you are unsure.

What if I'm not sure it's an incident?

Report it anyway. The IT Security Team would rather check a false alarm than miss a real attack.

Can I use my personal device on campus Wi-Fi?

Usually yes, but keep it updated, use a screen lock and avoid storing sensitive college data on it unless allowed by policy.
